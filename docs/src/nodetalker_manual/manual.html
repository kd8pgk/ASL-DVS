Chapter 1

What Node Talker is

A web page served by the Pi on port 8993 that lets a phone, tablet or computer listen to one node, transmit through it, and connect or tune a short list of favorites. Any browser will do; on Android, the optional Node Talker Mobile app (chapter 11) also keeps receive and transmit going with the screen off.

Receive

The node's audio, about half a second behind, cleaned up on the Pi (CTCSS and hum cut, levels evened out, squelch tails muted) and played in the browser with a live spectrum.

Transmit

Push-to-talk from the phone's microphone, processed in the page (filter, leveler, noise gate) and sent to a private node, 1921, that Node Talker keeps linked to your node.

Connect

Up to 15 favorites: AllStar nodes, EchoLink stations, DMR, STFU, YSF, P25, NXDN and D-STAR talkgroups and M17 reflectors, on its own or through the Dashboard.

Measure

Live meters, a mic test, a test through the node, and an Audio check that watches a few minutes of real use and says what to change.

1.1What it needs

An ASL3 node (Asterisk with app_rpt)Node Talker takes the node's audio from Asterisk and transmits through it.

1.2How it fits on the node

Node Talker adds two things to /etc/asterisk/rpt.conf, each with a backup first: one outstreamcmd line in your node's stanza, which sends the node's audio to Node Talker, and a private node 1921, which carries your transmissions in. Everything else stays as it was: the radio, the other links and DVSwitch hear and send exactly what they did before. Chapter 4 follows both paths end to end and takes the private node apart line by line.

Chapter 2

Install and first setup

Ten minutes from nothing to a first test transmission.

2.1Install

  1. The installer (7.2.0 and later) asks whether to add Node Talker; instmon (2.58.0 and later) lists it with the other tools and updates it. By hand: sudo python3 nodetalker_v….py --install. Over Node Listen, it moves the settings, favorites and certificate across and retires the old service.
  2. Open http://<pi-address>:8993. Node Talker moves you to https://. The certificate is made on the Pi, so the browser warns once: accept it.
  3. Log in with the Pi's root password.
  4. On Android you can use Node Talker Mobile instead of the browser: install it, add the Pi's address and log in on the page it shows (chapter 11).

2.2Receive: send the node's audio here

  1. Setup tab, open the RX card (Node receive). The table lists each node in rpt.conf and its outstreamcmd, if any.
  2. Pick your node and press Send its audio here. That node becomes Node Talker's main node.
  3. Press Restart Asterisk (it asks first; every link drops and reconnects).
  4. Listen tab: within a few seconds the status line shows a green feed light, and audio plays when the node passes any.
CAREFULA node takes one outstreamcmd. If yours already has one (a Broadcastify feed, for example), Node Talker refuses rather than replace it.

2.3Transmit: add node 1921

  1. Setup tab, open the TX: node 1921 card (Transmit node) and press Add node 1921. Node Talker adds a [1921] stanza (your node's template, a USRP channel, no hang time, telemetry, courtesy tone or station ID) and a [nodes] line, after a backup.
  2. Press Restart Asterisk. Within 20 seconds the card's summary reads Set up and linked: Node Talker links 1921 to your node and relinks it whenever it drops.

2.4First test transmission

  1. Setup tab, TX: node 1921 card, Microphone: press Test mic, talk for 4 seconds, and read the report (section 9.2). Adjust Mic gain until it says Good.
  2. Press Test through the node. It transmits for 4 seconds, over the air and to every link, and plays back what the node sent out.
  3. Listen tab: hold the push-to-talk button and make a short call.

4.2What happens when you press push-to-talk

  1. Press. The page (or the app) sends {"op":"key"} on the talk channel, the WebSocket /api/talk, and starts the microphone at once. Audio captured before the answer is held (up to half a second) so the first word isn't lost.
  2. Checks on the Pi. Node 1921 must be set up and linked; nobody else may be transmitting from Node Talker; with the busy lockout on, the node must be quiet (or your own unkey less than 1 s ago). Otherwise the answer is denied with the reason, and the page says why.
  3. Keyed. The answer {"ev":"keyed","tot":120} carries the TX time-out. The page turns red and counts; the held audio and every 20 ms frame after it go to the Pi.
  4. On the Pi. Frames collect in the TX buffer (120 ms by default), get TX gain if it is on, and leave every 20 ms as USRP packets with the key flag set, to 127.0.0.1:32018.
  5. On the air. Node 1921 hears a keyed channel, so it "receives"; the link carries that to your node, which keys its radio and sends the audio to every other link. Listen audio on your phone is muted meanwhile, and comes back 1 s after you let go.
  6. Release. {"op":"unkey"}. The Pi plays out what is left in the buffer, fades the last frame, sends three unkey packets, and answers {"ev":"unkeyed","why":"release"}.
  7. Or it stops by itself: at the TX time-out (timeout), when no audio has arrived for 1 s (deadman), when the talk channel closes (closed), or at the guard timer in tap mode.

4.3And on receive

Everything your node hears, including your own transmission as the node passes it, goes out through outstreamcmd to Node Talker, which cuts it into 20 ms frames, cleans it up once for everyone and streams it to each listener over /api/listen. Your phone buffers a quarter of a second and plays it. Chapter 5 has the details and the timings.

Chapter 5 · RX

Receive (RX) audio

From the radio to your ear: everything the node hears, cleaned up once on the Pi for every listener, then buffered, played and drawn by each phone.

5.1From the node to Node Talker

app_rpt mixes everything your node hears (the radio, every link, telemetry) and writes it, 8,000 samples a second at 16 bits, to the outstreamcmd program, a small part of Node Talker that passes it on to 127.0.0.1:6200. The audio pump cuts it into 20 ms frames (160 samples) and sends one to every listener every 20 ms. If audio arrives in bursts, up to 0.5 s waits in a queue; beyond that the oldest is dropped and counted.

The status line's feed light is green while frames arrive. The RX light and the busy lockout use the node's own level before any cleanup: a frame louder than −50 dBFS counts as audio, held for 0.6 s.

5.2RX cleanup on the Pi

Three steps, done once for every listener, change only what Node Talker's listeners hear. They are on by default; chapter 7 has each setting in detail. Nothing is done while nobody listens, and near-silent frames (under −65 dBFS) skip the work.

StepWhat it does to the sound
Cut below 300 HzRemoves hum and the lower CTCSS (PL) tones: 100 Hz drops by about 38 dB, 150 Hz by 24 dB, 250 Hz by 7 dB. Voice above 300 Hz is untouched.
Automatic levelAims speech at about −20 dBFS: brings quiet stations up by as much as 18 dB and loud ones down by as much as 6 dB. It turns down within a frame or two, comes up at 3 dB a second (faster for the first half second of a new transmission), and holds still between transmissions. RX gain (Audio levels) is added on top.
Soft limiterUp to about −1 dBFS nothing changes; above that, peaks are rounded off smoothly instead of clipped.
Squelch-tail gateMutes the burst of hiss after a station unkeys, and long silences. It tells hiss from voice by how alike neighbouring samples are: voice scores high, hiss low. A frame counts as hiss only when its samples one and two apart are both unrelated (flat hiss), so steady tones and loud "s" sounds are not muted. To mute a tail from its first frame, it holds the audio back 80 ms; when the gate opens it keeps the 40 ms before the opening and fades in, so the start of a word is not clipped.

5.3To your phone and out of the speaker

  • Format: 16-bit (128 kbit/s) by default, or μ-law (64 kbit/s) with 16-bit audio off. μ-law is the phone-line format: half the data, a little more hiss on quiet audio.
  • Listen buffer: the page plays each 100 ms of audio a fixed time after it arrives, 0.25 s by default, so a late packet doesn't make a gap. When audio arrives later than the buffer can cover, playback restarts (a dropout), and the Audio check counts it.
  • Smooth playback: the page upsamples 8 kHz to the phone's own rate (44.1 or 48 kHz) with a proper filter, which removes the faint sizzle some phones add.
  • Volume and then the speaker. The spectrum and meters see the audio before the volume.

5.4Where the half second goes

StageDelay
Asterisk to Node Talkera few tens of ms
Squelch-tail gate look-ahead80 ms (only with the gate on)
Page chunksup to 100 ms
Listen buffer250 ms by default (150 ms to 1 s)
Networka few ms on Wi-Fi at home; more on mobile data

The total is about half a second at home. This is why the node's own audio comes back late when you test through it, and why Node Talker ignores the busy lockout for 1 s after your own transmission.

Chapter 6 · TX

Transmit (TX) audio

From your mic to the air: shaped in the page, sent in 20 ms pieces, smoothed on the Pi and fed to node 1921, which is linked to your node.

6.1In the browser

  1. Browser processing. The phone's own Automatic gain, Noise suppression and Echo cancellation, each switchable. They run before Node Talker sees the sound.
  2. Mic gain, −12 to +24 dB: a plain volume control on the mic.
  3. Resample to 8 kHz with a filter that cuts at 3.4 kHz, so sounds above it don't fold back as hiss or whine.
  4. Voice filter (cuts rumble below about 250 Hz) or Radio voice (cuts below 300 Hz and lifts 2.5 kHz by 4 dB, a crisper, more "radio" sound). With Radio voice on, the voice filter is skipped.
  5. Noise gate (Off, Light, Strong): turns the mic down between words.
  6. Leveler: a gentle compressor that keeps your level even, followed by a limiter that never lets a peak past −1 dBFS.
  7. Every 20 ms (160 samples) a frame goes to the Pi over the WebSocket /api/talk.

6.2On the Pi and on the air

  1. TX buffer. The Pi waits until it has this much of your audio (120 ms by default) before it starts sending, so short hiccups on the phone's connection don't make gaps. If it runs dry anyway, it sends silence, refills, and counts a gap.
  2. TX gain (−12 to +12 dB, with a soft limit at about −1 dBFS), only with Adjust audio levels here on.
  3. The frames go as USRP packets to node 1921 (127.0.0.1:32018), keyed. On release the last frame is faded out and three unkey packets follow. If the audio has a gap, the last frame is repeated faded out instead of a hard cut, and the frame after the gap fades in.
  4. Node 1921 is linked to your node, so your audio goes out on the radio and to every link, like a local user's.

8.2Transmit, step by step

  1. Start clean. Microphone (TX card): Leveler on, Automatic gain off, Noise suppression on, Voice filter on, Noise gate Off, Mic gain 0 dB.
  2. Test mic. Hold the phone where you will hold it on the air and talk normally for 4 seconds. Targets: speech −24 to −12 dBFS, peaks −8 to −1 dBFS, limiter working under 20 % of the time. Change Mic gain by the amount the report suggests and test again until it says Good.
  3. Noise. If the report says Noisy, move to a quieter spot or closer to the mic first; then try Noise gate Light.
  4. Test through the node. For an exact comparison, turn Automatic level off for a minute (Audio levels). Came back should be within about 6 dB of Sent. If every phone comes back quieter or louder by the same amount, use TX gain; if the radio side is off, use SysMon's Tune tab. Turn Automatic level back on.
  5. On the air. Run an Audio check during a real contact or net (section 8.4) and apply what it suggests.

8.3Receive, step by step

  1. The node first. With the radio's own levels right (SysMon, Tune), the RX level in Audio levels (RX card) should peak around −10 to −3 dBFS on a strong local station.
  2. Look at the spectrum. A steady line or band below 300 Hz is a CTCSS tone or hum; with Cut below 300 Hz on it should be faint or gone. Thin bright lines right after transmissions are squelch tails: the gate should remove them.
  3. Leave Automatic level on. If speech is still quiet (the Audio check says so), raise RX gain by the amount it suggests.
  4. Dropouts. If audio breaks up on your connection, raise the Listen buffer a step.

8.6Common mistakes

  • Two automatic gains. The browser's Automatic gain and the leveler fight; leave the browser's off.
  • Mic gain into the limiter. More mic gain past the point where the limiter works most of the time adds distortion, not loudness. If the report says the limiter works more than 30 % of the time, turn it down.
  • Fixing the radio in Node Talker. RX gain and Automatic level only change Node Talker's listeners; a bad radio level still goes to every link.
  • Radio voice on a bright radio. If the radio adds pre-emphasis, Radio voice makes it shrill.
  • Judging levels through the speaker. Phone volume and speakers vary; trust the meters.

9.3The node test report

The test transmits for 4 seconds through the node and records what the node sends back out. The two strips are lined up so the same words sit above each other; Round trip is how long that took (about half a second at home). Difference compares the speech levels: within about 6 dB is fine.

Chapter 12

Troubleshooting by symptom

Each from the quickest check to the bigger fix. The Log card and SysMon's Node Talker card (its ASL-DVS tab) show what Node Talker and the node are doing.

No audio at all

  1. Status line: no feed yet means Asterisk isn't sending. RX card, Node receive: is the line on your node? Send its audio here, then Restart Asterisk.
  2. Feed green but silent: is the RX light on? Is Listen showing Listening.? Check the volume and the phone's own volume.
  3. Tap anywhere to start listening: the browser is holding sound until you tap.
  4. All 3 listener slots are in use: raise Max listeners or wait.

Audio breaks up or stutters

  1. Raise the Listen buffer a step (0.5 s on mobile data).
  2. Turn 16-bit audio off to halve the data.
  3. Move closer to the Wi-Fi access point. The Audio check counts dropouts.

Some stations are much quieter than others

Turn Automatic level on (Audio levels). If one station is quiet everywhere, it is that station.

Low hum or a tone under every transmission

Turn on Cut below 300 Hz. If the spectrum shows hum between transmissions too, it is on the node: check the radio interface's grounding.

A burst of hiss after every transmission

Set the Squelch-tail gate to Light, then Strong. The real fix is the radio's squelch or COS on the node sending it.

My transmissions are too quiet, too loud or distorted

Run the mic test and follow it (section 8.2). Distorted with a lot of limiting: lower Mic gain. Fine in the test but bad on the air: the node test, then TX gain or SysMon's radio levels.

Gaps or choppy audio in my transmissions

Raise the TX buffer to 200 ms; use Wi-Fi rather than mobile data. The Log shows gaps per transmission.

Push-to-talk is refused

MessageFix
The node is busyWait for quiet, or turn off the busy lockout if a constant noise keeps the node busy.
Someone else is transmittingWait; the button shows Busy with who and how long.
Node 1921 is not set up / not linked yetTX card, Transmit node: Add node 1921, Restart Asterisk, wait 20 s.
Tone test: the channel is busy / a callsign is needed / the test would last too longThe test waits for 3 s without audio above the activity level on the node, so wait for quiet (a constant noise above that level keeps it refusing). It identifies itself on the air, so set your callsign on the Digital card. It is also limited to 60 s and to the TX time-out less 5 s: use fewer tones or shorter ones.
Push-to-talk needs the https:// pageTurn HTTPS on and open the https:// address, or use Node Talker Mobile with app audio (chapter 11).
Microphone: Permission deniedAllow the microphone for the page in the browser's site settings.

Transmission stops by itself

Time-out: raise TX time-out. Audio stopped reaching the node: the phone's connection dropped; use Wi-Fi or raise the TX buffer.

Phone-specific

  • iPhone: sound needs one tap after the page opens; the lock screen keeps playing while the page is the active audio. Push-to-talk stops when the screen locks.
  • Android: in a browser, audio stops in the background. Use Node Talker Mobile (chapter 11), and set it to run in the background with its Battery button.
  • Bluetooth headsets switch to a low-quality call mode when the microphone opens; expect duller listen audio while you test or talk.
Chapter 13 TESTING

From the internet, and security

Node Talker is meant for your home network. Internet access is off unless you turn it on.

  • allow_wan in /etc/asl_dvs/nodetalker.conf (no checkbox; then sudo systemctl restart nodetalker). Off, clients from the internet get not open to the internet for every page. Home addresses, link-local and Tailscale (100.64.0.0/10) always count as home.
  • A Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale Funnel counts as the internet, whatever address it arrives from.
  • From the internet the password is only accepted on the https:// page.
  • On top of the per-address lockout, 20 failed internet logins in 10 minutes make every internet login wait until that window ends. Home logins are never held.
CAREFULThe login is the Pi's root password. Before opening Node Talker to the internet, make sure it is long and used nowhere else, and keep the Dashboard and SysMon on the home network.
